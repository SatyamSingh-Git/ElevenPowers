# Narrow ElevenPowers to the verdict that works

ElevenPowers is pointed at a real, measured problem, but its breadth has outrun its evidence. It now needs to narrow before it grows. The core idea is computed, model-free evidence on whether an agent's change is pinned by any test that would fail without it. That idea is sound, the field has not taken it, and it carries the project's one large pre-registered effect. On plain Claude Code (Sonnet 5.5 medium, 40 Python tasks), resolved patches with no pinning test fell from **84% to 37%** (p = 0.00016). That effect vanishes for superpowers users, who already write tests (**1 of 475 vs 0 of 472** resolved stages). The second positive result is that declared rule notes are acted on. A dependent file was lost **35 of 50 → 4 of 50** times with simulated perfect certification, and **31 of 50 → 15 of 50** with a model reviewer's certification. Everything else that has been measured has come back null or against the project:

- the coding-helper comparison against superpowers;
- the benefit to a model reviewer;
- the rule gate;
- the in-the-wild association.

Meanwhile the shipped surface keeps widening. It has five host adapters, but only Claude Code has been seen working live. It has three levels of test-strength instrument. On real agent changes, the coarsest one, the pinned verdict, was the only one to flag all eight unpinned cases. My recommendation is to stop building new instruments and new hosts. Put the pinned verdict where acceptance decisions are made, in this order:

1. Clear the honesty debt in the published claims.
2. Measure vacuity across the agents people actually use, at no model cost.
3. Prove the existing hosts live.
4. Ship the verdict as a pull-request check.
5. Extend function-level evidence to TypeScript and Java.
6. Only then return to research bets.

*Conventions.* "Measured" means a figure from the project's own records or a cited paper, with its stated bound. "Inference" means my reading of those figures. Internal sources are repository paths at HEAD `553a25c`. Literature figures marked snippet-grade came from search summaries and were not checked against the paper, following the project's own source-grading rule.

## One effect survived pre-registration, and it lives where superpowers is absent

**Measured.** The held-out vacuity sweep (2026-10-22/23) was sealed, never retried, and analysed with a plan committed before any run. Its design was 40 tasks from 12 permissive pure-Python repositories, two arms, three replicates each: 240 runs on Sonnet 5.5 at medium effort. Resolve rates did not differ: 88 vs 89 resolved, sign test p = 0.625. Among resolved patches, **74 (84%) were VACUOUS in the ordinary arm against 33 (37%) with ElevenPowers**. Across the 18 tasks whose arms differed, the assisted arm was lower on 17, with a per-task Wilcoxon **p = 0.00016** ([held-out sweep](../results/heldout/sweep-2026-10-22/README.md)). The mechanism is mundane. 106 of 107 VACUOUS patches contained no test file, and resolved ordinary patches added a test in 14 of 88 runs against 56 of 89 assisted. The record states its own bounds:

- one model, one host, Python only;
- small fixes, with a median of 10 changed lines;
- "not a population estimate";
- both arms carried the operator's own `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md`;
- VACUOUS "is not correctness".

The overhead was real. Wall time per run was 25.3 s vs 83.6 s, and model time 23.9 s vs 31.5 s, about 1.3×. The largest Stop phase, test strength at 18.3 s, is now off by default, but the saving is "an estimate from those records; no new sweep was run" ([overhead](../results/heldout/overhead/README.md)).

**Measured.** With superpowers installed in both arms, the same probe on the v3 snapshots found **1 VACUOUS stage of 475 resolved without ElevenPowers, and 0 of 472 with it** (p = 1.0). "Pinned" here is the coarse bar: at least one test fails with the whole change undone ([v3 vacuity](../results/multistage-v3/vacuity/README.md)). PLAN §12 had named "almost none of our recorded evidence is vacuous" as its most falsifying measurement, and records that falsifier as fired for superpowers users ([PLAN.md §12](../PLAN.md)).

**Measured.** Declared rule notes are the only author-side intervention that has shown an effect against superpowers. On five v3 stages chosen because v3 lost most there, with certification simulated as perfect, the dependent file was lost **35 of 50 times without the note and 4 of 50 with it** (exact one-sided stratified p = 8.6 × 10⁻¹³, cost +8.5%). With a blind, tool-free Sonnet 5.5 doing the certifying, it was **31 of 50 vs 15 of 50** (p = 7.3 × 10⁻⁵). A no-harm run on 53 other stages "rules out a harm of about 20 points, not a small one" ([rule groups acted on](../results/rule-groups-acted-on/README.md); [status 2026-11-04/05](../docs/status.md)). An instruction-worded note ("change both") was confirmed on one stage, **23 of 60 vs 5 of 60** lost (one-sided p = 8.3 × 10⁻⁵). On `claude-sonnet-5` the same comparison showed **0 of 40 vs 0 of 40**, so the benefit is stated for Sonnet 5.5 only ([status 2026-11-09/10](../docs/status.md)).

**Inference.** These two results share one shape: ElevenPowers helps where the agent would otherwise skip a step it knows how to do. A plain agent doesn't write a test; an agent doesn't know that a file is coupled to another. Superpowers already supplies the first habit, so the vacuity benefit is a substitute for practice its users have. The rule-note benefit depends on someone declaring the rule, and automatic discovery of rules has failed (next section). The README line "followed about 90% of the time, against about 30%" matches the perfect-certification run: 4 of 50 lost is about 92% kept, and 35 of 50 lost is 30% kept. It drops that run's bounds, which are simulated perfect certification, stages selected for their known losses, and one model ([README.md line 38](../README.md)). That line is the project's most exposed public claim.

## Every attempt to reach past the coarse verdict came back null

**Measured.** The pre-registered coding-helper comparison against superpowers (multi-stage v3, 2026-10-30) ran 12 synthetic cases × 2 arms × 5 replicates, with superpowers 7.0.0 in both arms, for $153.40. It lost earlier behaviour groups **29 times with superpowers alone and 35 with ElevenPowers added**, and left **43 vs 79** stage-summed stale documented facts. Both comparisons had Holm-adjusted p = 0.50. The measured superpowers loss rate was a third of the pilot's, so power was lower than planned: "a small benefit cannot be excluded. A large one is not supported" ([multistage v3](../results/multistage-v3/README.md)). The $0 diagnosis matters more than the headline. Of 31 losses in the ElevenPowers arm, 27 were never detected: 22 of those dependents import no edited file, and 5 sat behind the brief's "+N more" cutoff. The other 4 were detected and delivered but not acted on. A follow-up check found that rule proposals do not discover undeclared rules in real code. On 160 quiet co-changes from click, attrs, rich and pytest, proposals covered **60, the same as the control** ([PLAN.md §13 B](../PLAN.md)).

**Measured.** The reviewer-benefit study (2026-11-02, $5.23, pre-registered) gave a tool-free Sonnet 5.5 reviewer 21 seeded wrong changes. It caught **18 of 21 from the diff alone and 20 of 21 with the `ep_review --strength` report** (exact sign p = 0.5). False alarms on correct controls were 9 of 10 in both arms, and the diff-only arm was already at ceiling. The seeds were mutation-shaped, which favours the report, so the null is not a fluke against it ([reviewer benefit](../results/reviewer-benefit/README.md)). The rule gate sent back 7 runs that had left the dependent unchanged, and **converted 0 of 7**: each agent explained a scope choice and stopped ([rule gate](../results/rule-gate/README.md)). B8 showed that raw surviving mutants handed to Opus 5.5 produced tests that pin the implementation, including one that "killed" an equivalent mutant by asserting which slice expression the source uses ([B8 findings](../results/b8-feedback/findings.md)). The in-the-wild check covered 2,253 merged agent PRs in 165 repositories. It found a within-repository odds ratio of **0.91 (95% CI 0.54–1.54)** for follow-up fixes when no test was touched, with only 39% power at OR 1.5. Its exposure was "touched a test", not the revert probe ([wild follow-up](../results/wild-followup/README.md)).

**Measured.** The finest instruments add little over the coarsest. `ep_review` was run on agent changes from the held-out sweep, and eight of them were NOT PINNED. Line mutation named unpinned lines in 2 of the 8 and the TestMiner functions section in 1. "The review's own verdict, the coarsest of the three, is the only one that flagged all eight" ([review-functions](../results/review-functions/README.md)). The TestMiner rehearsals are "an illustration… not a rate" ([TestMiner](../results/testminer/README.md)).

**Inference.** The literature predicted most of this. Chen et al. found that prompting six models to write more or fewer tests changed test writing on 37–75% of tasks. It changed no model's resolution significantly (e.g. Kimi −2.6 pp, p = 0.228) ([arXiv 2602.07900](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07900v2)). Getting agents to write tests is therefore not where correctness comes from. Tufano et al.'s 29-expert experiment found that AI-flagged locations draw reviewers' attention and surface more low-severity issues, but no more high-severity ones ([arXiv 2411.11401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11401)). That matches the reviewer null. The industrial wins aim at the author, not the reviewer's verdict. Google found developers exposed to mutants write more tests, though the abstract gives no effect sizes ([arXiv 2103.07189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07189)). Meta's ACH got 73% of its mutation-guided tests accepted, after filtering equivalent mutants ([arXiv 2501.12862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12862)). The repeated "judgement, not oversight" result is consistent too: the rule gate's 0 of 7, and 4 delivered warnings ignored in v3. It suggests the remaining coding-helper losses are scope disagreements that only the user can settle, not information the hook failed to deliver.

## The field leaves the pinned check unclaimed, but not for long

**Measured.** None of the major AI reviewers surveyed states that it runs the project's tests against the reverted change or reports mutation survivors: CodeRabbit, Qodo, Cursor Bugbot/Graphite, Copilot code review, Claude Code Review, Greptile and Sentry Seer. Absence from vendor docs is not proof of absence. Execution is moving into review all the same. Copilot runs CodeQL and linters ([GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-03-05-copilot-code-review-now-runs-on-an-agentic-architecture/)). On 2026-11-18 CodeRabbit Verify began driving the running app through generated user journeys, with results tied to the tested commit and environment limits reported separately from failures ([CodeRabbit](https://coderabbit.ai/blog/introducing-coderabbit-verify)). That evidence model is close to PatchProof receipts. The category's measured weakness is noise. In 31,073 CodeRabbit review/feedback pairs from 239 repositories, **56.3% of comments were rejected**, mainly as invalid, redundant or out of scope ([arXiv 2607.03316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03316)). Pricing has gone usage-based: Claude Code Review averages $15–25 per review ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/code-review)), and a Bugbot run averages $1.00–1.50 ([Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/may-2026-bugbot-changes)).

**Measured.** The pieces exist elsewhere, but only in fragments. `unfaked` is an MIT, zero-dependency Claude Code Stop hook (PyPI 0.2.0, 2026-08-26, 1 star). It reverts changed source and reports added tests that still pass, with a self-reported 0 failures and 18 warnings over 240 maintainer commits ([unfaked](https://github.com/cmun2/unfaked)). Diffblue's free Test Quality Agent reports mutation score and "test strength" through the user's coding agent. It works per class or module, for Java and Python ([Diffblue](https://www.diffblue.com/resources/introducing-diffblue-test-quality-agent/)). Arcmutate posts surviving pitest mutants as PR comments for the JVM ([pitest blog](https://blog.pitest.org/dont-let-your-code-dry)). Meta's Just-in-Time catching tests use the same criterion, "fails on the change, passes on the parent", but to *generate* tests. Of 41 candidate catches reported, 8 were confirmed true positives ([arXiv 2601.22832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22832)). The problem is also common. In 86,156 agent-authored test-file patches, **80.2% carried weak or no explicit oracle**, by a regex classifier with no mutation scores. Strong-oracle rates on new test files ranged from 18% for OpenAI Codex to 67% for Claude Code ([arXiv 2606.18168](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18168v1)).

**Inference.** The pinned verdict alone is not defensible as unique, since a one-person project already ships it as a hook. A competitor with a sandbox could make it table stakes quickly; CodeRabbit already executes per PR. What ElevenPowers holds today is six things:

- the verdict computed rigorously, with pre-existing failures needing proof since 2026-11-06;
- receipts bound to a tree;
- project-independent runner discovery;
- zero token cost;
- five host adapters;
- a published record of its own false alarms and withdrawals.

That last asset answers the category's noise complaint directly, but only if per-state false-alarm rates are published where users see them. The "All Smoke" spread suggests vacuity is likelier on hosts other than Claude Code. If so, the held-out effect may be larger exactly where ElevenPowers is least proven.

## Breadth has outrun proof: one live host, Python-deep, no pull-request surface

**Measured.** Five adapters exist: Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Cursor Agent and Copilot CLI. Only Claude Code has passed installed, live-session acceptance, and the records disagree on how far. The status page says "1/10" host/language cells. The architecture card and the platform guide say 2/10, Claude Code with Python and with JavaScript, the architecture card adding that these "remain historical until requalified". Installed Codex trusted the hooks but "no callback was recorded", and "full native acceptance remains open on all hosts" ([status](../docs/status.md); [platforms](../the-guide/platforms.md)). Until 2026-11-10, the measured rule note "reached only Claude Code and Gemini": the Cursor and Copilot adapters dropped `additionalContext`, and Codex's `apply_patch` never requested a brief ([status 2026-11-10](../docs/status.md)). Every bundle needs Python 3.11+ on PATH ([platforms](../the-guide/platforms.md)). Language depth falls off quickly:

| Tier | What works |
| --- | --- |
| Python | Everything: parsing, review, mutation strength, TestMiner, call-level impact |
| JS/TS | Parsing, review, Stryker strength, import-level impact, V8 coverage capture; no function verdicts |
| Go, Rust, .NET, Ruby, PHP, Elixir | Counted parsing plus review |
| Maven/Gradle | Exit code only, with no command discovery |

The only GitHub surface is a job summary plus an artifact, with no Check Run, PR comment, SARIF, GitLab or MCP ([patchproof-github](../the-guide/patchproof-github.md)).

**Measured (external).** Claude Code (66%) and GitHub Copilot (59%) are the most-used coding agents in Stack Overflow's 2026 survey. The base population is not stated ([SO 2026](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/10/06/the-results-of-the-2026-developer-survey-are-here/)). Copilot's VS Code agent, the Copilot app and Copilot CLI "share the Copilot harness and its hook format" ([VS Code docs, 2026-11-18](https://code.visualstudio.com/docs/copilot/customization/hooks)). Zed and JetBrains host Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI and Copilot CLI through ACP ([Zed ACP registry](https://zed.dev/blog/acp-registry)). TypeScript became GitHub's most-used language in August 2025. Six languages (Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, C++, C#) account for nearly 80% of new repositories ([Octoverse 2025](https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-a-new-developer-joins-github-every-second-as-ai-leads-typescript-to-1/)). GitHub code scanning accepts SARIF 2.1.0 and turns its locations into in-file annotations. Uploading needs write access, and private repositories need Code Security ([GitHub docs](https://docs.github.com/en/code-security/how-tos/find-and-fix-code-vulnerabilities/integrate-with-existing-tools/upload-sarif-file)).

**Inference.** The adoption-weighted host list is the five already built, so the priority is live proof, not breadth. Proving the Copilot CLI adapter live is the cheapest path to the largest install base, VS Code agent mode, because the harness is shared. ACP extends the existing adapters to Zed and JetBrains for one validation run each. Windsurf, Cline, Kiro, OpenCode and Amp are long-tail. Reviewers live in the PR interface, and the one surface where the pinned verdict is uncontested is the one ElevenPowers does not deliver to.

## Recommended order: prove the verdict where acceptance is decided

The direction is right in its core and wrong in its spread. The 2026-11-05 internal synthesis already chose "from advice to gates, and from the reviewer to the author". It also noted that the test-discrimination check "answer[s] a factual question the agent can be wrong about", unlike the rule gate that failed ([audit 2026-11-05](../docs/research/audit_2026_11_05/README.md)). Since then, work has extended the reviewer report's instruments (TestMiner slices, `--functions`) instead of putting the verdict in front of more users. PLAN.md has no dated entry after 2026-11-01. Steelman of "park the project": superpowers (323,946 stars) already removes vacuity for its users, and hosts now ship their own review ([GitHub](https://github.com/obra/superpowers)). It fails on two facts. Plain-host users showed an 84% VACUOUS rate. And no reviewer product computes the revert verdict in CI. It would succeed if step 2 below finds vacuity rare across real agent PRs, which makes step 2 the decision point.

| Order | Move | Why | Gate before any benefit claim |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Honesty debt** (days). Add the bounds to the README "90% vs 30%" line. Reconcile 1/10 vs 2/10 acceptance. Correct PLAN's "3.1× model time" to wall time. Mark §12's "no better intermediate state" against the held-out 1-of-85 replay. Update the stale `held-out-benefit` card. Fix the three recorded but unfixed pre-existing-check defects (setup `ERROR`, `-x` kept on base, Windows backslash names). | Published claims are the project's credibility asset in a noisy category. | None; documentation and correctness only. |
| 2 | **No-model vacuity probe by agent.** Run `ep_review`'s revert verdict on the wild agent-PR corpus, split by agent (Codex, Copilot, Cursor, Devin, Claude). | Decides whether the 84% exists beyond plain Claude Code. The external 18–67% oracle spread says it may be larger elsewhere. | Pre-registered, as the wild follow-up was. |
| 3 | **Live acceptance on existing hosts.** Codex first (installed locally), then Copilot CLI, then the shared VS Code harness, then Cursor and Gemini. One ACP path. No new adapters. | Five claimed hosts with one proven is a liability. | Installed exercise per cell, watched both ways. |
| 4 | **Pull-request delivery of the verdict.** A Check Run carrying PINNED / NOT PINNED, plus pre-existing-failure proof. SARIF for unpinned lines and functions as secondary annotations. Optional comment. Then GitLab. An MCP server exposing `ep_review`/`ep_status` as a pull-mode complement. | The uncontested signal reaches the place decisions are made. Requires deciding to accept a write-scoped token, which the current design avoids. | Publish false-alarm rate per verdict state on maintainer commits, as `unfaked` does. |
| 5 | **Stop-time pinned gate for plain hosts.** Test-discrimination at completion, under `strict`. | The untested candidate the synthesis distinguished from the failed rule gate. | Paired, sealed run against plain Claude Code. |
| 6 | **Languages.** Java Surefire/Gradle count parsing and discovery (cheap). Then TS/JS function verdicts via V8 per-test runs. Then PIT and Stryker.NET as strength engines, borrowed rather than written. | TS is the most-used language; Java has the weakest support relative to use. | Run the real producer per runner before writing any parser. |
| 7 | **Research bets.** A patch-blind, issue-derived reproduction test confirmed by the revert check. Filtered, intent-targeted mutants for reviewers. A human-reviewer study on large diffs. | Otter's independent tests raised filtering precision from 60.8% to 91.9% at 33% recall ([arXiv 2502.05368](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05368v2)). Meta's equivalent-mutant filter reached 0.95/0.96 precision/recall after pre-processing ([arXiv 2501.12862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12862)). Agentic property-based tests found 56% valid bug reports (snippet-grade, [arXiv 2510.09907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09907v1)). | 50-task pilot first; outcome effect, not offline accuracy ([arXiv 2602.03338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03338)). |

**Stop or park, with reasons on record.**

- Automatic rule discovery and behaviour-diff detection: real-history proposals matched the control, and behaviour-diff would have caught 1 of 31.
- The rule gate: 0 of 7 converted.
- Further model-reviewer studies on small diffs: the reviewer is already at ceiling.
- New strength instruments until the coarse verdict's value is shown in a decision setting.
- New hosts beyond the five.

Keep the declared-rule note, and keep it honest. Its benefit depends on a person declaring the rule, and it was measured on Sonnet 5.5 only.

## Conclusion

The record changes what ElevenPowers should claim to be. It is not a coding helper; three months of sealed measurement found no benefit there against a strong baseline. It is not yet a reviewer aid either, because model reviewers are at ceiling and humans are unmeasured. What has held up is one cheap, factual verdict ("would any test notice if this change were undone?") and one cheap channel for the rules a person declares. The project's own measurement discipline is unusually strong. It found its own fabricated-looking figures, its harness leaks and an 18-second Stop phase no one read. But it now spends much of its effort calibrating instruments whose marginal value over the coarse verdict is the unproven part.

The window is also time-bound. The revert probe is simple enough that a solo developer shipped it as a hook, and CodeRabbit already runs sandboxes per PR. ElevenPowers' durable edge is the verdict computed rigorously, with receipts and a published false-alarm record, delivered on every major host and in the PR. Whether that edge matters at all hinges on one no-model measurement the project can run next: how often real agents' PRs, across vendors, arrive unpinned. If the answer resembles plain Claude Code's 84%, the narrowed plan above is the product. If it resembles superpowers' 1 in 475, parking the project is the honest outcome, and the published record of what didn't work is its contribution.
